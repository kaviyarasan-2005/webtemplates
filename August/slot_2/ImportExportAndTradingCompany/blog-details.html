Navigating the New Era of Nearshoring in North America

Sarah Jenkins CEO & Chief Strategist

As global volatility persists, manufacturing hubs are shifting closer to end consumers. The traditional model of relying entirely on trans-pacific ocean freight is being challenged by rising geopolitical tensions, unpredictable fuel costs, and the need for faster go-to-market speeds.

The Shift to Mexico

Over the last 24 months, foreign direct investment in Mexican manufacturing facilities has surged by 42%. Companies are realizing that the minor increase in labor costs compared to Southeast Asia is easily offset by the massive reduction in transit time—dropping from 30+ days via ocean freight to just 3-5 days via truck or rail directly into the US heartland.

"The question is no longer 'Should we nearshore?' but rather 'How quickly can we secure facility space before capacity maxes out?'" — EXIM Q3 Intelligence Report

However, nearshoring is not without its hurdles. The infrastructure along the border is currently straining under the increased volume. Wait times at major crossings like Laredo have seen a 15% increase year-over-year.

Building Resilience

The optimal strategy we recommend to our enterprise clients is a "China Plus One" model. Maintain core volume manufacturing in Asia where scale dictates lower unit costs, but establish a secondary node in North America for high-margin, time-sensitive goods.

At EXIM, our cross-border logistics teams are expanding capacity specifically to address this shift, offering consolidated LTL services from major Mexican manufacturing hubs directly to our US distribution centers.
