Trans-Pacific Rates: Why Q4 2026 Is Different

Marcus Reid
Chief Executive Officer

Spot rates on Asia–North America westbound lanes have climbed 34% since June, sitting at $3,840/FEU to the US West Coast as of this writing. The question every procurement manager is asking: is this Q3 season noise, or a structural shift?

The Three Pressure Systems

Unlike the COVID-era surge — which was a single demand shock — the current rate environment is the result of three overlapping pressure systems converging simultaneously.

1. Suez Rerouting Ripple Effects

Vessels rerouting around the Cape of Good Hope have added 10–14 days to Asia-Europe transit times. While this primarily affects E-W trade, the knock-on effect is fewer available vessels on Pacific lanes as carriers reallocate capacity to cover the Cape premium. We estimate 380,000 TEU of effective capacity has been absorbed by the rerouting globally.

2. Early US Inventory Build Ahead of Tariff Review

With a 90-day tariff review window expiring in November, US importers are front-loading inventory. SHIP's data shows a 28% increase in booked TEUs from clients sourcing from Greater China for September–October delivery compared to the same period in 2025.

3. Port Congestion at Gateway Hubs

Los Angeles/Long Beach berth utilisation currently stands at 91%. Every day a vessel waits at anchor costs carriers approximately $35,000–$80,000 per vessel per day depending on size. Carriers are pricing that risk into spot rates.

What Can Shippers Do?

There is no magic insulation from a supply-constrained market, but there are tactical positions available:

  • Lock forward contracts now. SHIP can offer 30-day rate locks. For 90-day windows, we recommend blended spot + contract positions.
  • Evaluate East Coast alternatives. All-water routes via Suez to the US East Coast are running $1,100–$1,600/FEU cheaper than West Coast on comparable commodity profiles.
  • Consider intermodal splits. Rail from Chicago to the East Coast post-arrival can change the total-landed-cost equation for Midwest consignees.

Our Forecast

We expect rates to stay elevated through October, with a possible correction of 10–15% in November as US inventory build plateaus and carriers redeploy the Cape-rerouted vessels back to Pacific lanes when the security environment improves. Do not expect 2023 lows; $2,800–$3,200/FEU is our Q4 floor estimate for the USWC.

"Rate locks and contract blends — not blind spot exposure — are how supply chain professionals navigate this market."

— Marcus Reid, CEO, SHIP

The bottom line: Q4 2026 is a volume-and-capacity squeeze, not a panic. Shippers who locked contracts in Q2 are sitting on $800–$1,200/FEU in savings per movement. For those still on spot, act now before the November peak-week surge.
