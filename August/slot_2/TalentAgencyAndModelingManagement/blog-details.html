From Audition Room
to Award Stage

Marcus Webb's decade-long climb — in his own words.

By James Caldwell • October 12, 2026
Marcus Webb photographed in London for MUSE Journal Cover Story, Autumn 2026.

Article Content

The first time Marcus Webb walked into a casting room, he had $200 in his bank account, a printed résumé, and exactly zero industry contacts. Ten years later, he has three film credits, a Netflix series deal, and a BAFTA nomination. He attributes all of it — every single opportunity — to learning the one skill most aspiring actors neglect: how to be unbookable in the room.

The Early Years — "I Was Terrible"

Marcus doesn't romanticize his beginnings. Born in Lagos to a civil engineer father and a drama teacher mother, he moved to London at 22 with a suitcase and a drama school acceptance letter. "My first open casting was for a deodorant commercial. I walked in, forgot my lines, knocked over the tripod, and walked out. I didn't get a callback. I barely got a goodbye."

What followed was two years of relentless rejection — 47 auditions, three callbacks, zero bookings. Then MUSE.

"My agent James Caldwell told me something I've never forgotten: 'You're not auditioning for the role. You're auditioning for the right to audition again.' That reframe changed everything."

The MUSE Method

Under MUSE representation, Marcus underwent a complete career overhaul — movement coaching, media training, a full portfolio rebuild, and crucially, access to the kind of auditions that don't appear on public casting boards. "MUSE submitted me to a BBC short that wasn't listed anywhere. I got the role. That short went to Toronto. That Toronto trip got me noticed by Meridian Pictures. That meeting is now a Netflix series."

The compound effect of an agent who believes in you, Marcus argues, is not just motivational — it's mathematically significant. "Every MUSE submission carries weight because casting directors know MUSE only sends talent they believe in. When my name appears in an envelope from MUSE, it's already pre-qualified."

On Discipline

Away from the spotlight, Marcus keeps a rigorous daily practice. Five miles every morning, two hours of script work, one hour of cold reads from scripts he's never seen. "I treat being an actor the same way a surgeon treats surgery. You wouldn't trust a surgeon who only practices when there's a patient. I practice every day, whether there's a role waiting or not."

"The camera sees everything you don't know. It sees the seams in your preparation. So I try to eliminate every seam."

What's Next

Meridian drops in January 2027. Marcus is already in callbacks for a feature film that would be, as he puts it, "the most ambitious thing I've ever done and the most terrified I've ever been." He says that like it's a good thing. With MUSE in his corner — and a decade of refusal to quit behind him — it probably is.
