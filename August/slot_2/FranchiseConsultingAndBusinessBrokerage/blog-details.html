The 2024 Main Street Multiples Report

Marcus Vance

The landscape of main street business acquisitions has shifted dramatically over the past 12 months. Driven by sustained interest rate hikes and tightening credit from SBA lenders, buyer behavior has evolved, forcing sellers to adjust their valuation expectations.

In this comprehensive Q3 report, we break down the empirical data regarding transaction multiples based on Seller's Discretionary Earnings (SDE) and EBITDA across key sectors.

The Macro Environment

Before diving into sector-specific multiples, it is vital to understand the macro factors depressing top-end valuations. The cost of capital is the primary culprit. When SBA 7(a) loan rates exceed 11%, the debt service burden on the buyer significantly reduces the amount of free cash flow left for owner compensation and business reinvestment.

"Valuations aren't dropping because businesses are performing worse; they are compressing because the cost of acquiring the cash flow has doubled."

Sector Breakdown: Q3 2024

1. B2B Services (Commercial Cleaning, IT Managed Services)

B2B services remain the darling of the main street market. Their recurring revenue models and generally lower capital expenditure requirements make them highly attractive in a tight credit environment.

  • SDE Multiples: 2.8x – 3.5x
  • EBITDA Multiples: 4.0x – 5.2x (for businesses > $1M EBITDA)
  • Trend: Stable. High demand for recurring revenue keeps multiples strong.

2. Retail & E-Commerce

Brick-and-mortar retail has seen the most significant multiple compression, particularly businesses heavily reliant on discretionary consumer spending. However, omni-channel retailers with strong e-commerce components are holding value.

  • SDE Multiples: 1.8x – 2.5x
  • Trend: Downward pressure. Buyers are demanding higher risk premiums.

3. Health & Wellness (Specialty Clinics, Boutique Fitness)

Healthcare continues to be insulated from broader economic shocks. Buyers, particularly private equity roll-ups, are aggressively pursuing profitable clinics.

  • SDE Multiples: 3.0x – 3.8x
  • Trend: Upward. Consolidation strategies are driving premium valuations for well-run operations.

The Rise of Seller Financing

Perhaps the most critical takeaway from Q3 2024 is the structural shift in how deals are funded. With bank lending tightening, deals are increasingly requiring seller financing to bridge the gap.

In 2022, a typical deal structure might involve 10% buyer equity and 90% SBA debt. Today, we are routinely seeing structures of 15% buyer equity, 70% SBA debt, and 15% seller note. Sellers unwilling to hold a note are seeing their businesses sit on the market significantly longer, or suffer deep discounts on the purchase price.

Conclusion

For sellers: The market is still active, and good businesses are selling. However, preparation is paramount. Clean financials, a defensible recast of SDE, and a willingness to participate in financing are key to maximizing your exit.

For buyers: It is a buyer's market for those with liquid capital. Deal flow is strong, and there are opportunities to acquire excellent cash flow at reasonable multiples, provided you can navigate the financing landscape.
