The Secret Behind Our 48-Hour Sourdough Dough

Marco Rossi Founder & Head Pizzaiolo
July 10, 2024 8 min read 24 comments 4,218 views

I'm often asked what makes our pizza different. People always assume it's the toppings — the quality of our mozzarella, the San Marzano tomatoes, the double-layered pepperoni. And those things matter enormously. But the real answer, the one that most surprises people, is much simpler: it's the dough.

More specifically, it's the 48 hours we give it before a single customer ever sees it. In this post, I want to share exactly what happens during those two days, and why every minute of that waiting is worth it.

Why Sourdough? Why Not Commercial Yeast?

When I first came to Foodville in 2003, I brought one thing with me from my mother's kitchen in Naples: a small jar of sourdough starter. This culture — which I have named "Nonna" — is now over 15 years old and is kept alive every single day at YUMZ.

Commercial yeast is fast. It raises dough in a couple of hours and it's predictable. But it produces a bread that tastes like... nothing particularly special. The bubbles are identical in size, the crust is soft, and the flavour is neutral.

Sourdough is different. The wild yeasts and lactic acid bacteria in the starter digest the flour slowly, producing complex organic acids (primarily lactic and acetic acid) that give the crust its distinctive depth of flavour — slightly tangy, slightly sweet, deeply nutty.

"You cannot rush fermentation. You can only create the right conditions and then get out of the way. The dough knows what it is doing."

— Marco Rossi, YUMZ Founder

The 48-Hour Process — Step by Step

Hour 0: The Mix

Every morning at 5AM, our kitchen team mixes a fresh batch of dough. The recipe is simple: strong bread flour (00 grade, 13% protein), water at precisely 18°C, sea salt, and the Nonna starter. That's it. No sugar, no oil, no additives.

The water temperature matters more than most people realise. Too cold and the fermentation is sluggish. Too warm and it accelerates too quickly, creating harsh flavours. We aim for 18°C every time.

Hours 0–4: Bulk Fermentation

The mixed dough rests at room temperature (around 22–24°C) for the first 3–4 hours. During this time we perform a series of "stretch and folds" every 30 minutes — gently pulling the dough from one side and folding it over itself. This develops the gluten structure without overworking the dough.

Hours 4–24: First Cold Retard

After bulk fermentation, the dough is divided into individual 280g balls and placed into covered containers. These go straight into the walk-in fridge at 4°C. The cold dramatically slows fermentation without stopping it entirely.

This is where the magic begins. The bacteria keep working, slowly. They break down complex starches into simpler sugars. They produce acids that strengthen the gluten and create flavour compounds that would never exist in a fast-risen dough.

Hours 24–48: The Finish

After 24 hours in the cold, we leave the dough for a full second day. When we pull it out on day two, the transformation is visible: the dough has developed a slight sheen, it feels alive in your hands — extensible but elastic, soft but strong. It smells like warm bread and faint yogurt. That's the lactic acid doing its work.

YUMZ Sourdough Base Recipe
Makes 4 medium pizza bases (280g each)
15 min
Active prep
48 hrs
Ferment time
4
Pizza bases
450°C
Bake temp

Ingredients

  • 600g strong bread flour (00 grade)
  • 390g cold water (18°C)
  • 12g fine sea salt
  • 120g active sourdough starter

Method (Summary)

  1. Combine flour and water, autolyse 30 min
  2. Add starter and salt, mix well
  3. Stretch and fold every 30 min × 4 times
  4. Divide into 280g balls
  5. Cold retard 48 hours at 4°C
  6. Remove 2 hrs before baking
  7. Stretch by hand, top, bake at max heat

Why We'll Never Change This Process

We've been asked many times to speed things up. Double the output by using commercial yeast. Cut the ferment to 24 hours. Use a dough sheeter instead of hand-stretching. Every time the answer is the same: no.

There is no shortcut that produces the same result. The 48-hour process is not a marketing gimmick. It's the reason YUMZ tastes the way it does. It's the reason people drive 40 minutes to get here. It's the reason our dough won the local food guide's "Best Pizza Base" award four years in a row.

Nonna — our 15-year-old starter — will keep being fed every morning. The dough will keep getting its 48 hours. And every pizza you eat at YUMZ will carry those two days of patience inside it, invisible but unmistakable in every bite.

Thank you for reading. Come in and taste the difference for yourself.
