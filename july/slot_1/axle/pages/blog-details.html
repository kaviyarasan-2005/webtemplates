5 Signs Your Brakes Need Attention

Jul 10, 2025 Safety 4 min read

Why Brake Maintenance Matters

Your vehicle's braking system is the single most important safety feature. Regular inspection and timely repair can mean the difference between a safe stop and a dangerous situation. Here are five warning signs every driver should recognize.

1. Squealing or Grinding Noises

A high-pitched squeal when you apply the brakes is often the first sign that your brake pads are worn. Most pads have built-in wear indicators that produce this sound. If you hear grinding, the pads are completely worn and metal is contacting metal, which can damage rotors.

2. Soft or Spongy Brake Pedal

If your brake pedal feels soft or sinks to the floor when pressed, this could indicate air in the brake lines, a fluid leak, or a failing master cylinder. This requires immediate professional attention.

According to the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration, brake-related issues account for approximately 22% of all vehicle crashes caused by mechanical failure.

3. Vehicle Pulling to One Side

If your car pulls to one side during braking, it may indicate uneven brake pad wear, a stuck caliper, or contaminated brake fluid. This inconsistency can compromise your ability to stop safely in emergency situations.

4. Vibrations During Braking

Feeling vibrations through the steering wheel or brake pedal when braking usually points to warped rotors. This is common with high-mileage vehicles and can be resolved with rotor resurfacing or replacement.

5. Dashboard Warning Light

Never ignore your brake warning light. It can indicate low brake fluid, worn pads, or a system malfunction. Have your vehicle inspected as soon as this light illuminates.

When to Seek Professional Help

If you notice any of these signs, schedule a brake inspection immediately. At AXLE, our complimentary brake inspection includes a full assessment of pads, rotors, fluid, and calipers with a detailed report.

  • Brake pads should be replaced every 25,000 to 50,000 miles
  • Brake fluid should be flushed every 2 years
  • Rotors typically last 50,000 to 70,000 miles
  • Always address warning signs promptly
