Top 10 Spring Flower Trends for 2026

Olivia Harper July 10, 2026 8 min read

Spring 2026 brings a refreshing wave of floral creativity. From garden-inspired loose arrangements to the bold use of texture and unexpected color combinations, this season celebrates nature's artistry in its most authentic form.

1. Garden-Style Arrangements

The structured, tight bouquets of yesterday are making way for loose, organic arrangements that look freshly gathered from an English garden. Think overflowing stems, varied heights, and a mix of blooms and foliage that feel effortlessly natural.

"The most beautiful arrangements are those that feel like nature herself placed every petal. Imperfection is the new perfection in floral design."

2. Dried Flower Accents

Dried flowers continue their popularity, but this spring they're being used as accents rather than standalone arrangements. A few sprigs of dried lavender or bunny tail grass mixed with fresh peonies creates a texture-rich, modern look that's both trendy and timeless.

3. Monochromatic Color Palettes

Single-hue arrangements are having a major moment. All-white, all-blush, or all-mauve bouquets create a sophisticated, gallery-worthy statement. The key is varying the flower types — mixing roses, ranunculus, and tulips in the same shade.

4. Sustainable Floristry

Eco-consciousness is shaping the floral industry. Locally sourced, seasonal flowers with minimal chemical treatments are preferred. Reusable containers, compostable wrapping, and foam-free mechanics are becoming industry standards rather than novelties.

5. Tropical Influences

Bold tropical elements like anthuriums, proteas, and monstera leaves are being paired with classic garden flowers for an unexpected contrast. The result is modern, dramatic, and utterly Instagram-worthy.

Whether you're planning a wedding, refreshing your home, or choosing a gift, these trends offer endless inspiration for creating meaningful floral moments this spring.

Lead Floral Designer at BLOOM with 12 years of experience in botanical artistry. Olivia specializes in wedding florals and seasonal design trends. She writes weekly for the BLOOM blog, sharing her passion for sustainable, beautiful arrangements.
