Navigating Roundabouts Safely

By James Miller • July 14, 2026 • 4 min read

Understanding Roundabout Traffic Rules

Roundabouts are designed to maintain continuous traffic flow and reduce severe intersection collisions. However, for learner drivers, roundabouts can feel chaotic due to the speed of approaching vehicles and the need for prompt decision-making. Passing a roundabout safely requires an understanding of lane positioning, signaling, and yielding priorities.

1. Approaching the Roundabout

As you approach, reduce your speed and identify the correct lane early. General lane usage is simple: if you are turning left (taking the 1st exit), stay in the left-hand lane. If you are turning right (taking the 3rd exit or making a full turn), stay in the right-hand lane. For going straight ahead (taking the 2nd exit), you usually enter in the left or middle lane, unless road markings indicate otherwise.

2. Yielding & Entering

Vehicles already inside the roundabout have the right-of-way. Always yield to traffic coming from your right. Wait for a safe gap in traffic before merging smoothly. Keep your speed controlled, and do not overtake vehicles within the roundabout lanes.

"Patience is your best defensive tool at a roundabout. Never force entry or cut off circulating vehicles."

3. Exiting and Signaling

Always use your turn indicators. When exiting, signal left immediately after passing the exit prior to the one you wish to take. This alerts drivers behind you and those waiting to enter that you are leaving the roundabout. Keep checking your mirrors for cyclists or motorbikes who might be in your blind spots.

By practicing these core guidelines under the support of a certified instructor, you'll quickly turn roundabout navigation into a natural, safe driving routine.
