How to Correctly Knock-In Your New Willow Cricket Bat

By Vikram Rajan • Oct 12, 2026 • 5 Min Read

Every cricketer knows the feeling of purchasing a new bat. The smell of the raw English Willow, the clean grains, and the potential for runs. However, taking a raw willow bat straight from the store wrapper to the pitch is the quickest way to crack the toe or split the edge.

Knocking-in is the essential process of compressing the soft fibers of the willow to make it strong enough to withstand the impact of a leather cricket ball travelling at speeds upwards of 120 km/h. Here is our master guide on how to complete the process correctly.

Step 1: Oiling the Face and Edges

Apply 1 to 2 teaspoons of raw linseed oil to the face, edges, and back of the bat. Use a soft rag or your fingers to spread it evenly. Avoid oiling the handle, the splice (where the handle meets the blade), or the labels.

"Over-oiling makes the willow soft and soggy, decreasing the bat's spring. Under-oiling leaves the wood dry and brittle."

Step 2: The Knocking-In Process

Using a heavy wooden bat mallet, gently strike the face and edges of the blade. Start softly, gradually increasing the force over 4 to 6 hours of work. Pay special attention to the edges and the toe, striking at a 45-degree angle to round off the edges and prevent split lines.

Step 3: Nets Practice Prior to Matches

Once mallet knocking is complete, take the bat to the nets. Face old, soft leather balls first, playing defensive shots. If no indentations or deep bruises appear on the face of the blade, the bat is fully compressed and ready for match leather.
