How to Correctly Knock-In Your New Willow Cricket Bat

By Vikram Rajan • Oct 12, 2026 • 5 Min Read
1. Oiling 2. Knocking 3. Net Testing

Every cricketer knows the feeling of purchasing a brand-new bat. The rich aroma of fresh English Willow, the pristine grain alignment, and the promise of boundary-clearing drives. However, taking a raw willow bat straight out of its store wrapper to a match pitch is the fastest way to crack a toe or split an edge on your very first ball.

Knocking-in is the mandatory conditioning process of compressing soft wood fibers, turning vulnerable raw willow into a dense, resilient blade capable of taking 120+ km/h leather ball impacts without fracturing.

"Over-oiling softens willow fibers and deadens stroke rebound. Under-oiling leaves the wood dry and fragile. Balance is everything."

Step 1: Oiling the Face & Edges

Apply 1 to 2 teaspoons of raw linseed oil to the face, edges, and back of the blade. Spread it evenly using a soft cotton cloth or your fingertips. Avoid oiling the handle splice, top shoulder stickers, or rubber grip.

Step 2: Automated & Manual Knocking

Using a heavy hardwood mallet, deliver controlled strikes to the face and rounded edges. Begin gently and gradually increase force over 4 to 6 hours of work. Strike edges at a 45-degree angle to round off sharp corners and build seam resilience.

Step 3: Net Testing & Final Seam Seal

Once mallet knocking is complete, take the bat into net sessions facing old leather balls first. If no deep indentations appear on seam impacts, your bat is fully hardened and match ready.
